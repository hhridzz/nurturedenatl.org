Veneers & Smile Makeovers

Veneers are thin, custom-made shells that bond to the front of your teeth to change their shape, colour, size or alignment. A smile makeover combines them with other treatments in one plan.

What it is

Porcelain veneers are made in a laboratory from ceramic. They resist staining and, with care, often last ten to fifteen years. Composite veneers are built directly on the tooth in a single visit, can be repaired more easily, and do not usually last as long.

A smile makeover is a plan for the whole smile. It can include whitening, veneers, crowns, gum reshaping and a professional clean, designed around your face, lips and gum line.

Who may need it

  • Stained teeth that do not respond to whitening.
  • Chipped, worn or slightly misshapen teeth.
  • Small gaps, or teeth that are mildly uneven or crooked.
  • Teeth of different sizes or lengths that spoil the symmetry of a smile.

Veneers are not ideal where there is active decay or gum disease, which should be treated first, where there is very little enamel, or where heavy grinding is not controlled. Badly crooked teeth are often better treated with aligners.

How it is performed

  1. Plan and design. We discuss what you want, take photographs and a 3D scan, and where possible let you preview the look before any tooth is touched. If you want whiter teeth, whitening is done first, because veneers cannot change colour afterwards.
  2. Prepare. A thin layer of enamel, often well under a millimetre, is removed so the veneer sits flush. Local anaesthetic is used if needed.
  3. Record. A scan captures the prepared teeth and temporary veneers may be fitted.
  4. Make. The porcelain veneers are crafted in a laboratory, which usually takes a week or two. Composite veneers are shaped directly on the teeth.
  5. Bond. The veneers are tried in, checked for colour and fit, then bonded with a light-cured adhesive. Your bite is checked and a review is arranged.

Alternatives

  • Teeth whitening, if colour is the only concern. It removes no tooth structure.
  • Composite bonding, which removes little or no enamel and can be repaired, but wears and stains sooner.
  • Crowns, for teeth that are badly broken down.
  • Clear aligners or braces, to correct crowding or gaps while keeping your enamel.
  • Gum reshaping, when the gum line rather than the teeth is the main issue.

Risks and limitations

  • Removing enamel is permanent. A prepared tooth will always need some form of covering.
  • Temporary sensitivity to hot and cold is common after preparation.
  • Veneers can chip, crack or come off, especially with nail-biting, biting hard objects, or grinding and clenching.
  • Decay can still develop at the edges, and the colour of a veneer cannot be whitened.
  • Veneers will need replacing in time. A single veneer can be hard to match to neighbouring teeth.

Recovery and aftercare

  • With temporary veneers, avoid sticky or hard foods.
  • New veneers can feel slightly bulky for a few days while your tongue adjusts. Mild sensitivity usually settles within days.
  • Do not bite nails, ice or pen tops, and do not open packets with your teeth.
  • If you grind your teeth, wear a night guard.
  • Brush, clean between your teeth and keep your cleaning and check-up visits. Composite veneers may stain, so go easy on tea, coffee and tobacco.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis. What is right for you depends on an examination of your teeth, gums, bone and general health, and your dentist will go through the options with you before any treatment begins.

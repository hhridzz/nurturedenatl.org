Dental Crowns & Bridges

A crown protects and rebuilds a tooth that is too damaged for a filling. A bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring a false tooth to the teeth on either side of the gap.

What it is

A crown is a tooth-shaped cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth, restoring its shape, strength and appearance. Crowns are made from ceramic, zirconia, metal alloys, or porcelain fused to metal.

A bridge is a fixed set of crowns joined to one or more replacement teeth. The crowns sit on the natural teeth beside the gap, and the false tooth between them fills the space. Bridges can also be supported by implants.

Who may need it

  • A tooth with a large cavity, a big old filling, or a crack or fracture.
  • A tooth that has had root canal treatment and needs protection.
  • Worn, misshapen or discoloured teeth that need rebuilding.
  • Someone missing a tooth whose neighbouring teeth are already heavily filled or crowned.
  • Someone who cannot or prefers not to have an implant, for reasons of bone, health, time or cost.

How it is performed

The details vary by tooth, but the gist is:

  1. Examine and plan. X-rays and an examination confirm the tooth can be restored.
  2. Prepare. Under local anaesthetic, decay or an old filling is removed and the tooth is shaped to make room for the crown. For a bridge, the teeth on both sides of the gap are shaped.
  3. Record. A digital scan, or an impression, captures the prepared teeth and your bite. We use 3D intraoral scanning where suitable, so there are no impression trays. The shade is matched to your own teeth.
  4. Protect. A temporary crown or bridge is fitted while the permanent one is made.
  5. Fit. The permanent restoration is checked for fit, bite and colour, then cemented.

Alternatives

  • Large filling, inlay or onlay, if enough healthy tooth remains to keep more of it.
  • Veneer, for cosmetic changes to a front tooth that is otherwise sound. See veneers and smile makeovers.
  • Dental implant, to replace a missing tooth without cutting into the teeth beside it.
  • Removable partial denture, which needs no cutting of neighbouring teeth but is less stable.
  • Resin-bonded bridge, which needs less cutting but is not as strong.
  • Leaving the gap, which is possible, but neighbouring teeth can drift and the opposite tooth can over-erupt, changing your bite.

Risks and limitations

  • Shaping the tooth removes healthy structure and cannot be reversed.
  • Temporary sensitivity to hot and cold. Occasionally the nerve is irritated enough to need root canal treatment later.
  • Decay can start at the edge of a crown, or under a bridge, if cleaning is not thorough.
  • Porcelain can chip, and crowns can loosen. If one anchor tooth of a bridge fails, the whole bridge may need replacing.
  • Crowns and bridges do not last forever. Ten to fifteen years is often quoted, but it can be shorter or longer depending on materials, bite, habits such as grinding, and hygiene.

Recovery and aftercare

  • Numbness usually wears off within a few hours. Avoid chewing until it has, so you do not bite your cheek or tongue.
  • With a temporary crown or bridge, avoid sticky or hard foods and floss carefully.
  • Mild sensitivity or a slightly different bite feeling is common for a few days after the permanent fit. If your bite feels high, come back so it can be adjusted.
  • Clean under a bridge daily with floss threaders, interdental brushes or a water flosser.
  • If you grind your teeth, ask about a night guard to protect the restoration.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis. What is right for you depends on an examination of your teeth, gums, bone and general health, and your dentist will go through the options with you before any treatment begins.

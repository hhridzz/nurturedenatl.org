Laser Dentistry & Gum Treatment

Dental lasers use focused light to treat gum and other soft-tissue problems precisely, often with less cutting and bleeding. We use a Nova laser system alongside conventional gum care.

What it is

A dental laser delivers a narrow beam of light energy that can reshape, trim or treat soft tissue. Our Nova laser works with four specific wavelengths of light, chosen for different tissues and tasks.

Gum treatment starts with understanding gum disease. Gingivitis is inflammation of the gums and is reversible. Periodontitis is when the infection has spread to the bone that holds the teeth. The foundation of treatment is thorough cleaning of the root surfaces plus good home care. A laser can be used alongside this to treat inflamed tissue, or on its own for minor soft-tissue procedures.

Who may need it

  • Bleeding, swollen or receding gums, or gum pockets found at a check-up.
  • An uneven gum line, or a gummy smile, where the gum can be reshaped.
  • Excess gum tissue around a tooth that needs a crown or filling.
  • A tight band of tissue under the tongue or lip (a frenum) that limits movement.
  • Darker gum patches that someone wishes to even out for cosmetic reasons.

How it is performed

  1. Assess. Your gums are measured and X-rays are taken if needed, to decide what you need.
  2. Protect and numb. Everyone in the room wears protective eyewear. Many soft-tissue procedures need little or no anaesthetic, and a numbing gel or injection is used when needed.
  3. Treat. The laser tip is guided along the gum line or into a pocket, or used to reshape tissue. For gum disease it is usually combined with a thorough cleaning of the roots.
  4. Finish. Small soft-tissue procedures often need no stitches. You leave with clear aftercare instructions and a review date.

Alternatives

  • Conventional deep cleaning (scaling and root planing), which remains the foundation of treating gum disease.
  • Scalpel (blade) surgery, often with stitches, for reshaping gums or releasing a frenum.
  • Gum surgery by a specialist, for advanced disease with bone loss.
  • Professional cleaning and home care alone, for mild gingivitis. See Guided Biofilm Therapy.

Research shows lasers can be a useful addition in some cases, but they have not been shown to replace thorough cleaning of the root surfaces in gum disease, and results vary. We will tell you honestly whether a laser is likely to help your situation.

Risks and limitations

  • Soreness, swelling or sensitivity for a few days.
  • Minor bleeding, which is usually less than with a blade.
  • Heat damage to tissue if settings or technique are wrong, which is why training and the right settings matter.
  • Gum disease can return if home care slips, and advanced disease may need more than a laser.
  • More than one visit may be needed. Please tell us if you are pregnant, take blood thinners, or have a medical condition.

Recovery and aftercare

  • Small soft-tissue procedures often heal faster, and mild tenderness usually settles within one to three days. Full healing often takes a week or two.
  • For the first day, eat soft, cool food and avoid anything spicy, hot or crunchy.
  • Brush gently and avoid the treated area on the first day. Rinse with warm salt water afterwards.
  • Keep up daily cleaning between your teeth. People treated for gum disease usually need regular professional cleaning for life.
  • Contact us if bleeding does not stop, swelling increases, or pain gets worse after the first few days.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis. What is right for you depends on an examination of your teeth, gums, bone and general health, and your dentist will go through the options with you before any treatment begins.

Guided Biofilm Therapy & Professional Cleaning

A professional cleaning removes the plaque, stain and hardened deposits that brushing and flossing cannot reach. At Nurture Dental we do this with Guided Biofilm Therapy, a modern, Swiss-developed protocol designed to be gentle and thorough.

What it is

Biofilm is the sticky, bacteria-rich layer that forms on your teeth every day. If it is not removed it hardens into calculus (tartar) and contributes to bleeding gums, bad breath, cavities and gum disease.

Guided Biofilm Therapy (GBT) is a structured way of cleaning it away. Rather than relying mainly on scraping with hand instruments, it uses a fine jet of warm water, air and a very fine powder to lift biofilm and stain from the tooth surface, and an ultrasonic instrument only where hardened deposits remain. A harmless disclosing colour is used first, so you can see exactly where the biofilm is.

Who may need it

  • Anyone due a check-up and clean. Most people benefit from a professional cleaning every six to twelve months, and some need it more often.
  • Bleeding, swollen or tender gums, or bad breath that does not go away.
  • Stains from tea, coffee, tobacco or paan.
  • Braces, clear aligner attachments, bridges or implants, where plaque is harder to reach.
  • Sensitive teeth, where traditional scaling can feel uncomfortable.
  • Anyone preparing for whitening, veneers or other cosmetic work, which go best on clean teeth.

How it is performed

The steps are adapted to each mouth, and not every visit needs every step. In outline:

  1. Check. Your teeth and gums are examined, including the depth of the pockets around each tooth.
  2. Disclose. A harmless colour shows where biofilm is sitting, which also helps you see where brushing misses.
  3. Clean. Warm water, air and fine powder polish biofilm and stain away from the teeth and the gum line. Where gum pockets are deeper, a thinner nozzle carries the same gentle powder below the gum line.
  4. Finish. Any remaining hard calculus is removed with a fine ultrasonic tip and water spray.
  5. Protect and plan. The teeth are checked again, protected, and you leave with a home-care plan and a suggested date for your next visit.

Alternatives

  • Conventional scaling and polishing with hand scalers, an ultrasonic scaler and polishing paste. It is effective and widely used, though some people find it less comfortable.
  • Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing), often with local anaesthetic, when gum disease has already formed deep pockets. GBT does not replace this when deeper treatment is needed.
  • Home care alone keeps plaque down, but it cannot remove calculus once it has formed.

Most of the stated advantages of GBT are about comfort and efficiency, and independent research comparing it with conventional cleaning is still developing. Both can give excellent results in skilled hands.

Risks and limitations

  • Mild gum tenderness or light bleeding for a day or so.
  • Temporary sensitivity, especially where gums have receded and roots are exposed.
  • Cleaning does not treat cavities or deep gum disease, and it will not whiten teeth beyond removing surface stain.
  • Please tell us about allergies, asthma or other breathing problems, heart conditions, and any medicines such as blood thinners before your visit, so the cleaning can be planned safely.

Recovery and aftercare

  • There is usually no downtime. Most people return straight to work and meals.
  • Gums may feel slightly tender for a day or two. Use a soft brush and rinse gently with warm salt water if they do.
  • Sensitivity usually settles within a few days. A desensitising toothpaste can help.
  • Brush twice a day, clean between your teeth daily, and keep your recall visits. Contact us if bleeding does not settle or pain increases.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis. What is right for you depends on an examination of your teeth, gums, bone and general health, and your dentist will go through the options with you before any treatment begins.

Replace a missing tooth with confidence.

Dental implants are titanium or other biocompatible fixtures placed in the jawbone to support a restoration. Depending on your circumstances, an implant may replace a single missing tooth, support a bridge or help stabilise a larger fixed restoration.

Long-term considerations

Implants do not decay like natural teeth, but they still need plaque control, professional reviews and monitoring for peri-implant disease. Smoking, gum disease, certain medical conditions and bite overload can influence outcomes.

The right solution for you

We also discuss tooth-supported bridges and removable options where relevant. Our goal is a stable, comfortable replacement that fits your anatomy and priorities, not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Restorative

The implant journey

From the first assessment to a natural-looking tooth.

  1. 01

    Assessment and 3D planning

    We assess the gap, adjacent teeth, gum health, bite, medical history and bone availability. A CBCT scan may be recommended to examine the site in three dimensions and identify important anatomical structures. Digital scanning supports restorative planning and communication with the laboratory.

  2. 02

    Implant placement

    The biocompatible fixture is placed in the jawbone, following the plan made in advance.

  3. 03

    Healing and integration

    Healing and integration are assessed. Timing differs between individuals, and immediate provisional teeth are considered only when stability and other clinical factors permit.

  4. 04

    Final restoration

    A custom abutment and crown are planned to harmonise with the surrounding teeth.
